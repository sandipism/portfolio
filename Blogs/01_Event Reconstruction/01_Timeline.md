# 01. Timeline

## Rasuwa–Bhote Koshi–Trishuli Disaster, 26–30 August 2026

### Scope
This timeline records the sequence of the 26 August 2026 event and the immediate 27–30 August response. It distinguishes observed/official timings from approximate or unresolved timing.

## 26 August 2026

| Time (NPT) | Event | Status / note |
|---|---|---|
| ~08:37 | Large seismic signal associated with the collapse was recorded. It was initially reported as a M4.4 earthquake; later USGS analysis attributed the signal to the massive collapse/debris event and estimated an equivalent magnitude of about M5.2. | Confirmed seismic signal; not treated as a tectonic trigger. |
| ~08:40 | Government/local reporting places the major Bhote Koshi/Lhende disaster onset at about this time. | Approximate. |
| ~08:40 onward | Large ice-rock/glacial mass failed at high elevation and generated an ice-rock avalanche into the Lhende river system. | Strongly supported/confirmed by later satellite and expert analysis. |
| Shortly after collapse | Avalanche material blocked the Lhende/Bhote Koshi river system on the China side and water accumulated behind the natural debris dam. | Strongly supported. Exact blockage duration unknown. |
| ~09:00 | Large flood entering Nepal from Tibet/Bhote Koshi was reported to authorities. | Technical report/reported sequence. |
| 09:05 | Information about the incoming flood was confirmed by the Flood Forecasting Division. | Reported technical timeline. |
| 09:15 | First downstream warning messages were issued. | Reported technical timeline. |
| 09:16 | Large-scale SMS dissemination through Nepal Telecom and Ncell was initiated, with about 679,295 messages reported. | Reported technical timeline. |
| ~09:15 | Nepal Red Cross reports sudden and intense surge entering the Bhote Koshi from the Tibet side. | Confirmed operational report. |
| 09:20 | Betrawati gauge last transmitted about 3.55 m before going offline. | Reported technical record. |
| 10:28 | Flood reached/approached Galchhi, Dhading; further downstream warnings issued. | Reported technical timeline. |
| 11:26 | Malekhu/Furke station crossed warning level. | Reported technical timeline. |
| 11:43 | Malekhu/Furke station crossed danger level; station/nearby bridge was subsequently destroyed. | Reported technical timeline. |
| 11:50 | Flood reached Malekhu Bazaar; the station had recorded about 10.8 m before being washed away. | Reported technical timeline. |
| 13:00 | Flood had passed Mugling; warnings extended toward Devghat and farther downstream. | Reported technical timeline. |
| 14:14 | Kalikhola station crossed the danger level of about 12.1 m and reached a reported peak of 12.3 m. | Reported technical timeline. |
| ~15:20 | Flood reached Devghat area. | Reported technical timeline. |
| ~16:00 | Devghat reportedly reached a peak water level of about 6.57 m. | Reported technical timeline. |
| ~18:30 | River level at Devghat reportedly returned toward normal, around 4 m. | Reported technical timeline. |

## 27 August 2026

- Satellite imagery and expert analysis provided stronger evidence for a large ice-rock/glacial collapse and avalanche at the source.
- Post-event imagery showed extensive debris and river-channel change.
- Two new/remaining water bodies were identified in the avalanche flow path / upper Lhende system and became a secondary hazard concern.
- Rescue, evacuation and damage assessment continued across Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading.
- Copernicus and Planet imagery became central to rapid assessment.

## 28 August 2026

- Continued monitoring of the upper Lhende water bodies and debris dams because of possible secondary outburst risk.
- Continued search, rescue, relief and access restoration.
- Satellite assessment of downstream channel widening and sediment deposition expanded.

## 29–30 August 2026

- Search and rescue continued, with difficult access, missing-person cases and body recovery extending far downstream.
- Authorities continued monitoring secondary upstream hazards and restoring critical access.
- Technical assessments were still evolving; final physical reconstruction remained pending.

## Important timing conclusion

The sequence **from the collapse to the downstream flood was extremely rapid**, but the exact duration of the initial Lhende blockage has **not been independently established**.

Do not state that the blockage lasted a fixed number of minutes unless a primary technical source or defensible remote-sensing/hydrological reconstruction establishes it.

### Core timeline

**High-mountain ice/rock collapse → ice-rock avalanche → river blockage → temporary water storage → blockage failure/release → debris-laden flood surge → Bhote Koshi → Trishuli → downstream impacts**
