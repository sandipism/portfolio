*This is Part 4 of a series reconstructing the Rasuwa–Bhote Koshi–Trishuli flood of 26 August 2026. Start with the [full overview](/blog/rasuwa-flood-2026-series-overview), or read [Part 3: Spatial Impacts](/blog/rasuwa-flood-2026-series-part-3-spatial-impacts).*

## Purpose

This part prevents early media reports, satellite interpretation and expert hypotheses from being treated as equally certain facts.

## Confirmed / high confidence

- A major ice-rock/glacial collapse occurred in the high Himalayan source area on 26 August 2026.
- The collapse produced a large ice-rock avalanche/debris movement.
- The avalanche entered the Lhende river system.
- The event produced a sudden, highly destructive downstream flood/debris surge.
- The Bhote Koshi–Trishuli corridor was severely affected.
- Major sediment deposition and river-channel change occurred.
- The collapse generated a large seismic signal. Later USGS interpretation attributes the signal to the collapse rather than a tectonic earthquake triggering it.
- Monitoring stations in the affected corridor were destroyed or lost data as the event passed.

## Strongly supported

- Avalanche debris temporarily blocked the Lhende/Bhote Koshi river system.
- Water accumulated behind the temporary natural dam.
- The temporary barrier subsequently failed and/or was overtopped.
- The resulting release combined stored water with large quantities of ice, rock and sediment.
- The flow entrained additional sediment and material downstream.
- The event was a cascading hazard involving multiple interacting processes.

## Reported, but verify against primary/technical documents

- Exact volume of the initial collapse.
- Exact location and dimensions of the initial blockage.
- Exact peak discharge at individual downstream locations.
- Exact building counts outside formal emergency-mapping products.
- Exact road/bridge damage totals as final figures.
- Exact travel time between each upstream and downstream location.

## Unresolved / do not state as fact yet

1. **Duration of the initial Lhende river blockage.**
2. Exact time at which the avalanche fully obstructed the river.
3. Exact time and hydraulic mechanism of the initial breach/overtopping.
4. Exact volume of water stored behind the first blockage.
5. Whether a pre-existing natural lake contributed directly to the initial surge, as opposed to the temporary lake created by avalanche blockage.
6. Exact proportions of water, ice, rock and sediment in the peak surge.
7. Exact amount of additional material entrained from the downstream valley.
8. Exact role, if any, of antecedent warming or other climatic factors in triggering the individual collapse.

## Important correction regarding the earthquake

The 08:37 seismic signal was initially treated in some reports as a M4.4 earthquake that may have triggered the avalanche. Later analysis revised this interpretation. The current evidence supports the seismic signal being generated by the massive collapse/debris event itself, with an equivalent magnitude around M5.2.

## Important terminology rule

Until the full technical assessment is complete, avoid treating the entire event simply as a **GLOF**.

Use terms such as:

- ice-rock/glacial collapse
- ice-rock avalanche
- temporary natural river blockage
- debris-laden outburst/flood surge
- cascading mountain hazard

## Confidence hierarchy

**Level 1:** Direct measurement / official technical record.

**Level 2:** Multiple independent expert or satellite sources agree.

**Level 3:** Single credible report / preliminary expert interpretation.

**Level 4:** Eyewitness/media statement requiring verification.

**Level 5:** Analytical inference made by us and not independently verified.
