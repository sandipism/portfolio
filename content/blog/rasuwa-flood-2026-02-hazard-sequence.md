*This is Part 2 of a series reconstructing the Rasuwa–Bhote Koshi–Trishuli flood of 26 August 2026. Start with the [full overview](/blog/reconstructing-rasuwa-bhote-koshi-trishuli-flood-2026), or read [Part 1: Timeline](/blog/rasuwa-flood-2026-series-part-1-timeline).*

## Working reconstruction

The August 26 event is best treated as a **cascading high-mountain hazard**, rather than as a simple rainfall flood.

## 1. High-mountain failure

A large mass of bedrock and glacial ice detached from a high-elevation slope in the upper Lhende catchment. Experts cited by the Kathmandu Post estimate the collapsed volume in the order of 100–200 million m³; the exact volume remains subject to refinement.

## 2. Ice-rock avalanche

The failed mass accelerated downslope and transformed into a high-energy ice-rock avalanche/debris flow. The avalanche travelled roughly 1–2 km vertically and entered the Lhende river system.

## 3. River blockage

The avalanche debris accumulated in the river, creating a temporary natural dam. This blocked the normal downstream flow and caused water to accumulate upstream.

**Critical unresolved issue:** the exact duration of this first blockage is not securely established.

## 4. Temporary impoundment and release

Water accumulated behind the debris dam until the barrier failed and/or was overtopped. The release mobilised the stored water together with ice, rock and sediment.

## 5. Debris-laden flood surge

The released flow was not a conventional clear-water flood. The avalanche supplied a very large initial sediment and boulder load, while the moving flow also entrained additional material from the valley and river channel.

The result was a highly destructive mixture of water, sediment, ice and rock.

## 6. Propagation through the Bhote Koshi

The surge entered the Bhote Koshi system and rapidly affected Rasuwagadhi, Timure and surrounding settlements.

## 7. Propagation through the Trishuli

The flow continued downstream into the Trishuli, where it affected settlements and infrastructure through Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading and farther downstream.

## 8. River-channel and sediment response

The event produced extensive:

- sediment deposition
- bank erosion
- channel widening/change
- debris deposition
- infrastructure scour and damage

Downstream satellite comparisons show that the affected river corridor became much wider at many locations.

## 9. Secondary hazard phase

After the initial flood, additional/remaining water bodies formed or persisted in the avalanche pathway. These became a secondary concern because another breach could generate another downstream surge.

## 10. Working cascade

```text
High-mountain bedrock/glacial failure
        ↓
Ice-rock avalanche
        ↓
Avalanche enters Lhende Khola
        ↓
Temporary river blockage
        ↓
Water accumulation
        ↓
Blockage failure / overtopping
        ↓
Water + ice + rock + sediment surge
        ↓
Bhote Koshi
        ↓
Trishuli
        ↓
Downstream flood/debris impacts
        ↓
Secondary debris dams / water bodies
        ↓
Continuing secondary-hazard risk
```

## Interpretation to preserve for later analysis

The event may be better understood as a **cascade of interacting hazards**. The immediate destructive process was not generated by rainfall-driven runoff alone. This distinction matters for later work on hazard attribution, EWS and infrastructure design.
