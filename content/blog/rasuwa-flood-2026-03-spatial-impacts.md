*This is Part 3 of a series reconstructing the Rasuwa–Bhote Koshi–Trishuli flood of 26 August 2026. Start with the [full overview](/blog/reconstructing-rasuwa-bhote-koshi-trishuli-flood-2026), or read [Part 2: Hazard Sequence](/blog/rasuwa-flood-2026-series-part-2-hazard-sequence).*

## Source-to-impact corridor

The event should be assessed as a connected river-corridor disaster rather than as isolated municipal incidents.

```text
Upper Lhende
   ↓
China-side blockage/source zone
   ↓
Rasuwagadhi
   ↓
Timure
   ↓
Syabrubesi / upper Bhote Koshi corridor
   ↓
Bhote Koshi
   ↓
Betrawati / Gerkhu
   ↓
Trishuli Bazaar
   ↓
Devghat
   ↓
Malekhu / Benighat and farther downstream
```

## 1. Upper Lhende source area

Dominant processes:

- glacial/bedrock failure
- ice-rock avalanche
- river blockage
- temporary water storage
- major debris deposition

This is the **hazard source zone**.

## 2. Rasuwagadhi

Among the first major impact areas. Reported impacts included destruction of border/customs facilities, roads, bridges, buildings and critical infrastructure.

The destructive flow included very large amounts of sediment and debris.

## 3. Timure

Severe damage to settlement and infrastructure was reported.

Copernicus EMS assessed the Timure–Syabrubesi area using very-high-resolution post-event imagery. Its published building-damage analysis reported hundreds of affected/destroyed buildings and destroyed bridges within the mapped areas. These are image-based emergency-mapping findings and should not be confused with a final field-verified inventory.

## 4. Syabrubesi

Severe settlement and infrastructure impacts were reported. Copernicus EMS identified extensive mass movement and numerous affected buildings in its mapped area.

## 5. Bhote Koshi–Trishuli transition

Downstream, the event remained strongly sediment-laden. The river system transported water, rock, sediment, vegetation and structural debris into the Trishuli corridor.

## 6. Betrawati / Gerkhu

The river corridor widened markedly after the event and extensive sediment deposits were visible in 27 August satellite imagery.

The Betrawati–Rasuwagadhi road corridor also experienced major disruption.

## 7. Trishuli Bazaar

Downstream settlement areas were affected by the flood and debris. Reports describe building and bridge destruction and disruption of critical infrastructure.

## 8. Devghat

The downstream flood continued into the Devghat area. Water levels remained significant enough to produce a measurable flood pulse and continuing debris/sediment impacts.

## 9. Malekhu / Benighat and farther downstream

The downstream river corridor recorded major water-level rises and continued transport/deposition of sediment and debris.

A colleague's satellite-width analysis between Betrawati and Benighat found the following at 13 selected locations:

- January 2026 average observed width: ~100 m
- 28 August 2026 average observed width: ~350 m
- Average observed width ratio: ~3.8

Some points showed increases of more than five times.

**Methodological caution:** these are selected cross-river observations of the post-event riverbed/channel footprint. They should not be interpreted automatically as a uniform increase in bankfull width or as a direct measure of peak discharge.

## Spatial pattern

### Upper corridor
Highly concentrated, high-energy avalanche/debris impacts.

### Middle corridor
Direct flood/debris destruction to settlements, roads, bridges and hydropower infrastructure.

### Lower corridor
Broader river-channel change, sediment deposition and downstream flood impacts.

## Important spatial questions for later analysis

- Where was the highest concentration of sediment deposited?
- Where did the channel permanently or temporarily change?
- Which settlements became isolated because access routes were cut?
- Which infrastructure nodes were exposed to the combined flood/debris process?
- How far downstream can the distinctive sediment/debris signal be traced?
